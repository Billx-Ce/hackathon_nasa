Analyzed candidates

Each window measures 10 · 10 km and uses 500 m cells. La Joya and Atacama are two source regions; the two Atacama candidates do not constitute two independent training regions.

What PCA learns

PCA summarizes seven variables: robust relief, elevation spread, mean slope, P90 slope, slope spread and two roughness measures. It learns combinations of these variables from Earth training windows. Distance in this summary ranks descriptive similarity to Jezero.

The baseline uses all seven standardized variables without compression. Distances are dimensionless, not percentages or probabilities. Each subset is centered on its own mean; absolute elevations are not compared across planets.

Internal evaluation and method limitations

Explained variance describes how much variation the summary retains, not scientific accuracy. Agreement with the baseline does not demonstrate improvement. Training and evaluation windows are separated by a spatial buffer but may retain autocorrelation. There are only two independent Earth regions.

There are no expert labels or external validation. Scientific accuracy has not been calculated. This evaluation does not demonstrate geological equivalence, suitability for building a base or safe rover routes.

Sources, scales and provenance

Mars · Jezero

NASA/MGS MOLA · USGS distribution. Approximately 463 m per pixel at the equator, with elevation in meters relative to the Martian areoid. Supports regional terrain studies, not small obstacles or safe rover routes.

Earth · La Joya and Atacama

Mapzen Terrain Tiles / Skadi: global mosaic, elevations in meters relative to the WGS84/EGM96 geoid. SRTM/GMTED2010 courtesy of USGS; ETOPO1, NOAA. Individual pixels are not attributed to a specific SRTM version. Source attributions and terms.

DEMs were averaged into local AEQD projections with 500 m cells. Mars uses a sphere of radius 3,396,190 m; Earth uses WGS84. A common cell does not equalize effective resolution or eliminate instrumental uncertainty. Metrics are computed from DEMs, not the exaggerated mesh.

NASA POWER provides historical Earth environmental averages to explore test conditions; not Martian climate or ranking variables. The map uses Natural Earth, public domain.
