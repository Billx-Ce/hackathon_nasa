INTERPRETING THE RESULTS

What does this comparison tell us?

Reading the analyzed candidates…

We compare only three fixed windows from two Earth regions, at a regional scale of 10 km. Lower distance means greater similarity in the analyzed variables; it is not a percentage, probability or suitability for building a base.

Scale and differences between sources

Both subsets use 500 m cells and the same physical extent. MOLA has approximately 463 m pixels at the equator; the Earth mosaic uses different instruments and resolution. Aggregating to a common cell does not equalize effective resolution. Each window mean is removed to compare terrain variation rather than absolute planetary elevations. The vertical control only changes the visualization.

02 / UNSUPERVISED LEARNING

Candidates in perspective

PCA + DISTANCE

Three predefined windows from two regions. Ranking depends on seven descriptors; these are not the three best locations on Earth.

Calculation and evaluation

PCA summarizes seven terrain variables into patterns learned from Earth windows. A smaller distance to Jezero indicates greater similarity in that summary.

Explained variance describes how much variation the summary retains, not scientific accuracy. Agreement with the baseline does not demonstrate improvement. There are only two independent regions and neighboring windows retain autocorrelation. Internal evaluation does not validate geological equivalence or suitability for building a base.

SCIENTIFIC SCOPE

These indices are not probabilities of planetary equivalence or certifications. MOLA supports regional terrain studies, not small obstacles or safe rover routes. Matching cell sizes does not remove differences in instruments and processing.

About the project

Academic prototype for NASA Space Apps. Challenge: “Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars”. Current scope: Mars and Earth candidates for exploring potential test sites.
